## 1. OutboxEvent privacy (reconciles v4.4 §1.1 with v4.5 §6.1) [TR-11, TR-23 revised]

**The contradiction (C-53):**
- v4.4 §1.1 allowed a plaintext recipient user ID and aggregate ID in OutboxEvent.
- v4.5 §6.1 classified OutboxEvent as "soft reference: pseudonyms and envelopes".

v4.6 adopts the pseudonymised form throughout.

### 1.1 What an OutboxEvent row may contain

| Column | Content | Plaintext? |
|---|---|---|
| `id` (UUIDv7), `event_type`, `category` (SECURITY \| FINANCIAL \| MATRIMONIAL_ACTIVITY \| ACCOUNT \| SYSTEM), `status`, `attempts`, `lease_token`, `lease_expires_at`, `created_at` | Queue facts | Yes |
| `recipient_pseudonym` | The recipient account's `SubjectPseudonym`. NULL for system events. | Yes (a random UUID) |
| `dedupe_key` | `HMAC-SHA256(K_dedupe, business event identity)`, `UNIQUE` | Yes (not reversible) |
| `aggregate_type` | For example `MatrimonialRequest` | Yes |
| `payload_enc` | Aggregate ID and any handler parameters, encrypted with the **queue key** `K_queue[v]` (AEAD, with `id` as associated data), plus `queue_key_version` | **No.** Ciphertext only. |

**Banned:** raw user, profile or resource IDs; names; phone numbers; message text.

**Why a queue key and not the recipient's subject key:** financial events (`REQUEST_REFUND_DUE`) must still run after the subject's key has been destroyed (v4.5 §1: financial events are never cancelled). A system-held queue key keeps them processable.

**Queue key lifecycle:** each version is wrapped by the KEK and rotated monthly. A version is destroyed once every row encrypted with it has been purged (outbox retention of 30 days, plus the maximum retry window). After that, any copy of those rows in a backup can't be decrypted.

### 1.2 How handlers resolve recipients and aggregates

1. Decrypt `payload_enc` to get the aggregate ID.
2. Resolve `recipient_pseudonym` through `SubjectPseudonym`, requiring `subject_id IS NOT NULL`. The outbox worker role has SELECT on that table only, and every read is counted in a metric.
3. Reload the current records and re-check eligibility with the single predicate in §5 (v3: handlers re-check eligibility).

**If the recipient can't be resolved** (unlinked, or deletion is in progress): a notification-class event becomes `CANCELLED (RECIPIENT_UNAVAILABLE)`. A financial event doesn't need a recipient to run (the refund targets the ledger account taken from the aggregate). Only its notification step is skipped.

### 1.3 Deletion and redaction

- **At deletion PROCESSING:** the account's undelivered notification-class events → `CANCELLED`. Financial events stay as they are.
- **Redaction trigger** (replaces v4.4's "payload immutable"; C-59): `payload_enc`, `recipient_pseudonym` and `dedupe_key` can **only** change from a value to NULL, and only when status is `DONE`, `CANCELLED` or `DEAD`. Any other change is rejected.
- **Redaction schedule:** the retention job redacts rows 7 days after they reach a terminal status, and purges them at 30 days.

### 1.4 Pseudonyms are created eagerly (C-57)

The `SubjectPseudonym` and `SubjectDataKey` rows for an account or profile are created **in the same transaction that creates that account or profile**. Staff accounts are covered by a one-off backfill at G4. Business transactions then only **read** pseudonyms.

**Why:** creating them lazily inside business transactions (an insert with a unique-index wait at level 8) could deadlock when two transactions created pseudonyms for the same pair of subjects in opposite order.

**Tests:**
- T-OUT-001: a schema and content test finds no raw IDs in OutboxEvent.
- T-OUT-002: a refund event is processed after the sender's subject key was destroyed.
- T-OUT-003: an unresolvable recipient → notification CANCELLED, financial event still completes.
- T-OUT-004: the redaction trigger rejects a non-NULL change and a change on a non-terminal row.
- T-OUT-005: after the queue-key version is destroyed, an old row can't be decrypted.
- T-OUT-006: every account and profile creation path also creates its pseudonym and key in the same transaction.

## 2. External compliance registry privacy (revises v4.5 §2.1) [TR-24 revised]

### 2.1 Content: pseudonyms only (C-54)

| Entry | Fields |
|---|---|
| `SHRED` | `op_id`, `pseudonym`, `key_id`, `kek_version` |
| `DELETION_COMPLETED` | `op_id`, **`account_pseudonym`, `profile_pseudonym`** (the random UUIDs from `SubjectPseudonym`), `completed_at` (day precision) |
| `MAINTENANCE` | §3 / §6 (contains no personal data) |

The registry stores **no** account ID, profile ID, deletion-request ID, phone, email or name. An internal reference such as a deletion request is represented only by `op_id` (a random UUIDv7) and the pseudonyms.

### 2.2 How a restore finds the right deletions without the registry identifying anyone

- A pseudonym means something **only** together with a `SubjectPseudonym` row that still has `subject_id` set.
- In the **live** database, deleted subjects have been unlinked, so the registry resolves to nobody.
- A **restored backup** taken before the deletion still holds that link. The restore process matches `account_pseudonym` against the restored `SubjectPseudonym` and re-runs the idempotent deletion steps for that subject (v4.5 §2.1 step 3), which unlinks it again. A pseudonym that isn't in the restored database is skipped, because that subject didn't exist when the backup was taken.

So the registry on its own is just a list of random UUIDs. It becomes identifying only next to a backup that already contains the identities, and backups expire.

### 2.3 Two streams with different retention, plus checkpoints (C-55)

| Stream | Entries | Object-lock retention |
|---|---|---|
| **R (restore)** | `SHRED`, `DELETION_COMPLETED` | **Longest backup retention + 30 days.** Once no restorable backup predates an entry, it can't be needed. |
| **M (maintenance)** | All maintenance operations (§6) | The maintenance-audit retention placeholder (7 years, subject to legal review) |

Each stream has its own `seq` and hash chain. One chain with mixed retention would break verification once the shorter-lived entries expired.

**Checkpoints:** every day, a signed `CHECKPOINT {stream R, seq, head_hash}` entry is written to stream **M**, so it's retained long-term. Verifying stream R starts from the newest checkpoint older than the oldest restorable backup. Expired R entries before that checkpoint don't break verification.

**Test:** T-REG-010 (§3.6) checks registry content and finds no account, profile or deletion-request IDs and no direct identifiers.

## 3. Registry and maintenance-log consistency [TR-24 revised]

### 3.1 Unique maintenance operations

**`maintenance_operation`** is a mutable state table, separate from the append-only audit log:

| Column | Notes |
|---|---|
| `op_id` | PK, UUIDv7, generated by the job **before** it starts |
| `stream` | R or M |
| `kind` | §6 catalogue |
| `target_digest` | SHA-256 of the canonical target, for example a partition name or a pseudonym |
| `status` | `INTENT → REGISTERING → REGISTERED → APPLIED → OUTCOME_REGISTERING → VERIFIED`, or `ABORTED` |
| `intent_seq`, `intent_hash`, `outcome_seq`, `outcome_hash` | Registry positions and hashes |
| `attempts`, `last_error`, timestamps | — |

```sql
UNIQUE (kind, target_digest) WHERE status <> 'ABORTED'
```

This stops two live operations from acting on the same target.

### 3.2 Serialised sequence numbers and hash chain

- **`registry_stream_head`:** `(stream PK, last_seq, last_hash, pending_op_id NULL)`.
- **Allocating an entry** takes `FOR UPDATE` on the head row:
  - **Refused** while `pending_op_id` is set to a *different* operation. So each stream has at most one external write in flight.
  - Otherwise: `seq = last_seq + 1`, `prev_hash = last_hash`, `entry_hash = SHA-256(canonical entry including prev_hash)`, set `pending_op_id`, set `last_seq` / `last_hash`, store `seq` and `hash` on the operation. Commit.
- **Confirmation** (after the external write succeeds) clears `pending_op_id`.

### 3.3 Concurrency between maintenance jobs

- Each job holds a **session-level advisory lock** `pg_advisory_lock(NS_MAINT, hash(stream))` while it works on that stream.
- On top of that: the partial unique index (§3.1), the single pending entry per stream (§3.2), and a **conditional external create** (`If-None-Match: *` on object key `<stream>/<seq>`). Two writers therefore can't use the same `seq`.
- **Maintenance tables are their own lock domain.** No business transaction touches them.
- Inside an apply transaction the lock order is: operation row → stream head → target objects (partition DDL locks, or `SubjectPseudonym` / `SubjectDataKey` rows).

### 3.4 Write order: external registry first

**Rule:** the database never reaches an irreversible state the registry doesn't already record.

**Stream R** (shred, completed deletion), one entry per operation:
1. Insert the operation (`INTENT`).
2. Allocate `seq` (→ `REGISTERING`).
3. Conditional put of the entry to the registry (→ `REGISTERED`, pending cleared).
4. Apply transaction (destroy key, unlink, local cache, maintenance-log row) → `APPLIED`.
5. Verify (§3.5) → `VERIFIED`.

There's no outcome entry: applying is idempotent, and the reconciler finishes it.

**Stream M** (partition and hold operations, key lifecycle), two entries per operation:
1. Allocate and put the **INTENT** entry (→ `REGISTERED`).
2. Apply transaction (v4.5 §5.2 move-and-drop, plus the local `maintenance_audit_log` INTENT/APPLY row). **In the same transaction**, allocate the OUTCOME `seq` (→ `OUTCOME_REGISTERING`).
3. Put the **OUTCOME** entry (`COMPLETED`, with row counts and `ids_digest`).
4. Verify → `VERIFIED`.

**If the apply transaction fails for good:** allocate and put an `OUTCOME = ABORTED` entry (→ `ABORTED`). The chain has no gaps and no dangling INTENT.

### 3.5 What "complete" means

An operation is `VERIFIED` only when all of these hold:
- (a) Every registry entry the operation needs exists, and a read-back of its hash matches the hash stored in the database.
- (b) The operation's effect is confirmed by its own check: the partition is gone and the hold-table count equals the recorded count; or the key is tombstoned and the pseudonym unlinked; or the deletion steps are complete.
- (c) The local `maintenance_audit_log` has the matching row(s) with the same `op_id`, `seq` and hash.

### 3.6 Retry and reconciliation after a partial failure

The reconciler runs hourly, at startup and after a restore. It holds the stream advisory lock.

| Found | Action |
|---|---|
| `REGISTERING` | Read `<stream>/<seq>`. Present with matching hash → `REGISTERED`. Missing → put again (same bytes). Present with a **different hash** → **integrity alarm, the stream halts.** |
| `REGISTERED` (not applied) | Re-run the apply transaction (idempotent). If it can't apply, move to the ABORTED path. |
| `APPLIED` / `OUTCOME_REGISTERING` | Put the outcome again, as above |
| Anything not `VERIFIED` | Run verification. After N failures, alert and leave it for an operator. |
| **After a restore** | Stream M and R entries newer than the backup are compared with the restored state. Their effects are re-applied (deletions, shreds, partition drops). Missing `maintenance_audit_log` rows are **inserted** with `restored_from_registry = true` (append-only compatible). |
| Nightly | Both streams are verified end to end (seq continuity, hashes, signatures) against the database |

**Failure-injection tests (both directions):**

| ID | Injection | Pass condition |
|---|---|---|
| T-REG-001 | **Registry written, database not updated:** crash after the external put, before `REGISTERED` is committed | The reconciler sees the object with a matching hash → `REGISTERED` → apply → `VERIFIED`. One effect only. |
| T-REG-002 | **Database applied, registry outcome missing:** crash after the apply commits, before the OUTCOME put | The reconciler puts the outcome → `VERIFIED`. No duplicate `seq`. |
| T-REG-003 | External put keeps failing | Stays `REGISTERING`. **No database effect.** Alert. The stream accepts no other operation. |
| T-REG-004 | Apply transaction fails permanently after INTENT | `ABORTED` outcome written. No gap. The target is unchanged. |
| T-REG-005 | An object already exists at `seq` with a different hash | Integrity alarm, stream halted, nothing applied |
| T-REG-006 | Two jobs start on the same stream | Serialised. `seq` is contiguous. |
| T-REG-007 | A second live operation on the same target | Rejected by the partial unique index |
| T-REG-008 | Restore a backup older than N maintenance and M deletion operations | All re-applied. Maintenance-log rows restored from the registry. The application stays down until this finishes (v4.5 startup gate). |
| T-REG-009 | An entry altered or removed in the registry | Detected by nightly verification and by the restore check |
| T-REG-010 | Content scan | No account, profile or deletion-request IDs or direct identifiers in any stream |

## 4. UUIDv7 and partition boundaries (corrects v4.5 §5.1) [TR-23 revised, TR-13]

### 4.1 Version-specific generation (C-56)

- **PostgreSQL 18:** use the built-in **`uuidv7()`**.
- **PostgreSQL 15/16:** use the custom implementation below. It's RFC 9562 layout: 48-bit Unix millisecond timestamp, version 7, 12-bit `rand_a`, variant `10`, 62-bit `rand_b`. It needs no extension, because `gen_random_uuid()` is built in from PostgreSQL 13.
- **Portable column default:** every table uses `uuid_v7()`. A migration creates `uuid_v7()` as a wrapper around `uuidv7()` when `server_version_num ≥ 180000`, and as the custom body otherwise.

```sql
CREATE OR REPLACE FUNCTION uuid_v7() RETURNS uuid
LANGUAGE plpgsql VOLATILE PARALLEL SAFE AS $$
DECLARE
  ts_ms bigint := floor(extract(epoch FROM clock_timestamp()) * 1000)::bigint;
  b     bytea  := uuid_send(gen_random_uuid());          -- 16 random-ish bytes (v4)
BEGIN
  b := overlay(b PLACING substring(int8send(ts_ms) FROM 3 FOR 6) FROM 1 FOR 6); -- 48-bit ts, big-endian
  b := set_byte(b, 6, (get_byte(b, 6) & 15)  | 112);    -- version nibble 0111
  b := set_byte(b, 8, (get_byte(b, 8) & 63)  | 128);    -- variant bits 10
  RETURN encode(b, 'hex')::uuid;
END $$;
```

That leaves 74 random bits (12 in `rand_a`, 62 in `rand_b`). Uniqueness is enforced by the primary key, not assumed.

### 4.2 `uuid_v7_lower(ts)`

This returns the **smallest valid UUIDv7 for the millisecond containing `ts`**: timestamp bytes, version nibble 7, every random bit zero, variant `10`.

```sql
CREATE OR REPLACE FUNCTION uuid_v7_lower(ts timestamptz) RETURNS uuid
LANGUAGE sql IMMUTABLE STRICT PARALLEL SAFE AS $$
  SELECT encode(
           substring(int8send(floor(extract(epoch FROM ts) * 1000)::bigint) FROM 3 FOR 6)
           || '\x70008000000000000000'::bytea,          -- 70 00 | 80 00 00 00 00 00 00 00
         'hex')::uuid
$$;
```

**Why the boundaries are correct:** PostgreSQL compares UUIDs byte by byte, unsigned, and the timestamp is in the most significant 6 bytes. So:
- every v7 UUID with timestamp ≥ T sorts ≥ `uuid_v7_lower(T)`, because with an equal timestamp, byte 6 is `0x7?` ≥ `0x70`, byte 7 ≥ `0x00`, and byte 8 is in `0x80–0xBF`, which is ≥ `0x80`
- every v7 UUID with timestamp < T sorts below it

`extract(epoch FROM timestamptz)` doesn't depend on the session timezone, so `IMMUTABLE` holds. Boundaries are always written as UTC literals.

### 4.3 Partitions

```sql
CREATE TABLE audit_log (..., id uuid NOT NULL DEFAULT uuid_v7(), ...,
  CONSTRAINT audit_log_id_is_v7 CHECK (
     substring(id::text, 15, 1) = '7' AND substring(id::text, 20, 1) IN ('8','9','a','b')),
  PRIMARY KEY (id)) PARTITION BY RANGE (id);

CREATE TABLE audit_log_2027_01 PARTITION OF audit_log
  FOR VALUES FROM (uuid_v7_lower('2027-01-01 00:00:00+00'))
             TO   (uuid_v7_lower('2027-02-01 00:00:00+00'));   -- TO is exclusive
```

- No DEFAULT partition. Partitions are created 3 months ahead (v4.5).
- The CHECK rejects non-v7 UUIDs, which could otherwise be routed to the wrong partition.
- **CI runs on PostgreSQL 16 and 18**, covering both code paths. AS-3 is unchanged (≥ 15 supported).

**Tests:**

| ID | Check |
|---|---|
| T-UUID-001 | 100,000 generated IDs all have version 7 and variant `10` |
| T-UUID-002 | Extracted timestamp is within ±5 ms of `clock_timestamp()` |
| T-UUID-003 | 1,000,000 IDs, no duplicates |
| T-UUID-004 | **Property test:** for random T, `uuid_v7_lower(T)` ≤ a synthetic v7 at T with every random bit 0 or 1; and > a synthetic v7 at T−1 ms with every random bit 1 |
| T-UUID-005 | **Boundary routing:** synthetic IDs at `2027-01-31 23:59:59.999Z` (random bits all 1) → January partition; at `2027-02-01 00:00:00.000Z` (all 0) → February partition. Also tested at Dec → Jan and Feb 29 (2028). |
| T-UUID-006 | On PostgreSQL 18, built-in `uuidv7()` IDs pass the CHECK and route the same way |
| T-UUID-007 | A non-v7 UUID is rejected |
| T-UUID-008 | A duplicate ID is rejected by the primary key across partitions |
| T-UUID-009 | Session `TimeZone='Asia/Karachi'` doesn't change any boundary |
| T-UUID-010 | `uuid_v7_lower` is deterministic and IMMUTABLE |

## 5. One eligibility predicate for notifications (refines v4.5 §3.3) [TR-03 revised]

### 5.1 Single source: `get_account_standing(account)`

It lives in the central eligibility service (TR-03), reads the authoritative records, and **is the same predicate** used by:
- the account-state gate (v3 §8.4)
- v3 §9 step 8 (delegated access)
- the recovery-scope login (v4 §10.6)
- notification eligibility

| Standing (first match wins) | Source |
|---|---|
| `DELETED` | `AccountDeletionRequest.status = COMPLETED` |
| `DELETION_PROCESSING` | … `= PROCESSING` |
| `DELETION_RECOVERY` | … `= PENDING_RECOVERY` |
| `RESTRICTED_FULL` | An active `UserRestriction(FULL_ACCOUNT)`. This is an account-level suspension. |
| `RESTRICTED_LOGIN` | An active `UserRestriction(LOGIN)` |
| `DEACTIVATED` | `User.deactivated` / `is_active = false` |
| `ACTIVE` | None of the above |

**Profile-level conditions** are a separate input: profile SUSPENDED, PAUSED or CLOSED; a profile-level restriction; a restrictive identity hold (v4 §11.2). Write transactions evaluate standing under User `FOR SHARE` (and profile `FOR SHARE` where relevant). Delivery handlers evaluate it again.

### 5.2 `can_notify(account, category, profile=None)` [REC-BIZ, OD-02e new sub-item]

| Category | Allowed standings | Profile conditions |
|---|---|---|
| **SECURITY** (sign-in, password, session revoked, deletion requested or cancelled) | All except `DELETION_PROCESSING` and `DELETED`. Security notices can't be turned off in preferences [DECIDED]. | Ignored |
| **ACCOUNT** (deletion-recovery reminders, restriction notices) | `ACTIVE`, `RESTRICTED_*`, `DEACTIVATED`, `DELETION_RECOVERY` | Ignored |
| **FINANCIAL** (refund returned, payment confirmed) | **`ACTIVE` only** | **Ignored:** credits belong to the account (FR-SUB-011), so a suspended or held profile doesn't hide account finance notices |
| **MATRIMONIAL_ACTIVITY** (interests, requests, messages, connections) | `ACTIVE` only | The profile must be `ACTIVE`, with no profile-level restriction and no restrictive identity hold. PAUSED → suppressed. |

Ordinary preferences (channel AND category) are applied after this check, as before.

**Effect on refunds (reconciles v4.5 §3.3):** the "credit returned" event is created only if `can_notify(debit_account, FINANCIAL)` is true. In every other case the refund is still recorded and shows in the account's financial history.

**Tests:** **T-NOT-001…028** cover every standing × category combination. Plus:
- T-NOT-029: a restricted profile still receives FINANCIAL notices.
- T-NOT-030: a standing change between creation and delivery → suppressed at delivery.
- T-NOT-031: the gate, step 8 and notifications all call the same predicate (an architecture test).

## 6. Maintenance audit coverage and recovery [TR-24 revised]

### 6.1 Every maintenance operation, and where it's recorded

| Kind | Stream | Database record | Registry entries |
|---|---|---|---|
| `SHRED` | R | Operation + `maintenance_audit_log` (APPLY) | 1 |
| `DELETION_COMPLETED` | R | Operation + log (APPLY) | 1 |
| `PARTITION_CREATE` | M | Operation + log (INTENT, APPLY) | INTENT + OUTCOME |
| `PARTITION_DROP` (includes hold copy) | M | Same | INTENT + OUTCOME (with counts and digest) |
| `HOLD_RELEASE` | M | Same | INTENT + OUTCOME |
| `KEK_ROTATE`, `KEK_DESTROY` | M | Same | INTENT + OUTCOME |
| `QUEUE_KEY_ROTATE`, `QUEUE_KEY_DESTROY` | M | Same | INTENT + OUTCOME |
| `RESTORE_RECONCILE` | M | Same | INTENT + OUTCOME |
| `REGISTRY_CHECKPOINT` | M | Log (APPLY) | 1 |
| `MAINT_LOG_PARTITION_DROP` | M | Recorded in the **current** year's maintenance partition | INTENT + OUTCOME |

**Rule:** no maintenance action may run outside the §3 protocol. Code review enforces this, and a startup check confirms every maintenance command is registered in the catalogue.

### 6.2 Recovery procedure

The §3.6 reconciler, and after a restore:
1. Stop the application.
2. Restore the database.
3. Verify streams R and M.
4. Re-apply stream R.
5. Re-apply the effects of stream M OUTCOME=COMPLETED entries newer than the backup.
6. Insert the missing `maintenance_audit_log` rows (`restored_from_registry = true`).
7. Write `restore_reconciled_seq` for both streams.
8. The startup gate checks both values.
9. Start traffic.

**Retention:**
- The database maintenance log and stream M share the placeholder retention (7 years, legal review).
- Stream R follows §2.3.
- Dropping a maintenance-log partition is itself an M-stream operation, recorded in the current partition and in the registry.

**Tests:** T-REG-001…010 and T-LOG-001…010 (v4.5), plus:
- **T-REG-011:** an operation kind not in the catalogue is refused at startup.
- **T-REG-012:** after a restore, every M operation newer than the backup has its effect re-applied and its log row restored.
